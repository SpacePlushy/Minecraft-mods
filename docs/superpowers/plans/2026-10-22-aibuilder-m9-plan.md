# AI Builder M9 (Ready-Made Parts, Wiring and the Calculator) Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:executing-plans (Native, as M1–M8) to implement
> this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** "a working calculator" builds a redstone machine that adds two numbers from 0 to 15, set on two rows of four
levers, and shows the sum on two seven-segment digits, every sum right: Claude puts it together from a library of
tested parts (`place_gadget`) and a `wire` tool, tests it, and the live check reads the digits itself.

**Architecture:** As M1–M8: pure `core`, thin world side. Every gadget is generated by pure Java as blocks in a
gadget-local frame plus named ports, then placed through the same queue, validation, box, protection and undo as any
write tool. Combinational gadgets (adder, digit, number) come from one generator, a programmable logic array (PLA):
products found by Quine–McCluskey, laid out with a small set of redstone taps that are correct by construction, so
every function is wired the same proven way. Small machines (clock, pulse, toggle, latch, counter, piston door) are
fixed layouts. `wire` lays dust, supports and repeaters between ports and refuses to touch other redstone.

**Tech Stack:** as M8 (Minecraft 26.3, Fabric, Java 25, JUnit 6).

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-22-aibuilder-v2-design.md` §5.3, §5.4 (M9), §5.6 (M9), and §3's decisions.
The v1 spec and M8's rules still hold.

## Design calls this plan makes (the spec leaves them to it, §6)

- **Gadget frame.** A gadget is built facing forward in its own frame: gx right, gy up, gz forward, `(0, 0, 0)` its
  front-left-bottom block. `place_gadget(kind, at, facing, options)` puts that corner at `at` and turns the gadget so
  its forward is `facing` (forward, right, back, left), turning direction words in block states with it. Inputs are
  on the front, outputs on the back, unless a gadget says otherwise.
- **Ports.** A port is the spot where a wire's end dust goes, just outside the gadget, with a buffer repeater one step
  inside: an input's faces in, an output's faces out. So a port always takes or gives a full-strength signal, any wire
  shape joins it, and nothing inside a gadget ever touches a wire. The answer lists every port by name with its
  position in the build's frame, the gadget's box, its block count and how many ticks it takes to settle.
- **The PLA** (`core/gadgets/Pla`), layers by gy: 0 output supports, 1 output dust, 2 product supports and OR taps,
  3 product dust, 4 input supports and AND taps, 5 input dust. Inputs enter at gy 5 through the front: for input i a
  true line along gz at gx 4i and its complement at 4i+2 (a dust branch into an inverter block with a wall torch).
  Product t is a dust row along gx at gz c0+2t. AND tap: a wall torch beside an input line's support, right above
  the product row, off while the line is on; so a row carries NOT(AND of the lines it taps). OR tap: a wall torch on
  a product row's support, right above an output line, on while the product is true. Output lines run along gz under
  the AND plane (pitch 2) and leave through a buffer repeater at gy 1. Air above every torch; repeaters only where no
  tap needs the dust (input lines at odd gz, outputs at even gz, product rows none: at most 4 inputs, so every OR tap
  is within 14 of every AND tap). The physics rests on 26.3's code: a wall torch powers dust right below it, a block
  under dust counts as powered for a torch on it, dust ignores blocks powered only by dust (checked with javap).
- **Which way things face** (26.3's code, checked in M8's review): a repeater's or comparator's `facing` names its
  input side, so a gadget's repeater carrying the signal forward faces back; an observer's names the side it watches;
  a wall torch's points away from its block. The generators take the signal's direction and write the state from it.
- **Gadget tests** run in a bigger empty structure (`gadgets`, 48 × 16 × 64, a stone floor) with an allowed box to
  match, through the same queue as builds (ArenaBuilds with a box), so the shape and power-up passes run as in a build.
- **Logic** (`core/gadgets/Logic`): Quine–McCluskey primes, a greedy cover shared across outputs, don't-cares allowed;
  measured sizes: full adder 7 products, BCD digit 9 (10–15 don't care), 0–3 digit 4, binary-to-decimal of 4 bits 15.
- **Kinds** (options in brackets, all checked; anything else is refused):
  - `lever_row` [bits 1–8, label up to 8 characters, default "in"]: levers on posts with a sign each (the label and
    the bit's value 1, 2, 4…); outputs `<label>0…` on the back at pitch 4, gy 1.
  - `lamp_row` [bits 1–8]: inputs `in0…` on the front at pitch 4, gy 1.
  - `adder` [bits 1–8]: full-adder PLA cells side by side at pitch 14, the carry hopping from each cell's front to
    the next one's carry port; inputs `a0…`, `b0…` on the front at gy 5; outputs `s0…s<bits>` on the back at gy 1.
  - `digit` [none]: a 4-bit BCD code `d0…d3` (front, gy 5) shown on a 5×5 seven-segment face of lamps. The PLA's 7
    outputs climb behind it, turn and run forward over its top in a bundle (3 columns 2 apart, lines stacked 2 apart,
    so none touch), each into a repeater behind its segment's centre lamp; a lamp strongly powered by a repeater
    lights the lamps beside it too, so the corners join. The face is at the front, above the inputs, facing forward.
  - `number` [bits 1–5]: a binary number `n0…` shown on one or two digits (0–31): a PLA works out tens and the upper
    ones bits (bit 0 passes by), feeding a 0–9 digit and a 0–3 digit, faces side by side at the front-top. It does
    what the spec's `to_decimal` + `digit` do; `to_decimal` is not a kind of its own (ruled in the ledger).
  - `clock` [period 8–64 ticks, even]: a torch and a repeater loop, a square wave; input `hold` stops it.
  - `pulse` [length 2–8 ticks]: a comparator subtracting a delayed copy of its input: a pulse on each rising edge.
  - `toggle`: a waxed copper bulb read by a comparator: each pulse flips the output.
  - `latch` [bits 1–8]: data repeaters locked from the side by `hold`: outputs follow `d…` until `hold` turns on.
  - `counter` [bits 1–8]: a ripple of waxed copper bulbs, each fed the inverse of the one before; input `count`.
  - `piston_door` [2×2]: four sticky pistons in a 4-wide wall frame, open while `open` is powered. 3×3 is not offered
    (it needs slime or double extenders, which the machine rules forbid or which are fragile; ruled in the ledger).
- **The session remembers its gadgets** (`BuildSession.gadgets`: kind, options, at, facing; saved; none in older
  saves), so refinements and tests know them, and anything derived (ports, display lamps) is recomputed from the pure
  generator.
- **`wire(from, to, via)`.** A single wire from an output port to an input port: from each point to the next it runs
  along z, then along x, climbing or dropping a level a block from the start of the run until it's at the next
  point's height. `from` and `to` may instead be equal-length rows of ports (no `via`): each wire runs along z to its
  own turning row, along x, then along z, the rows 2 apart in the order that keeps them from crossing (right shifts
  rightmost first, left shifts leftmost first); too little room between the rows is refused with the room needed.
  Dust on a support (stone where the spot below is air), a repeater facing the signal's way at least every 15 blocks
  on a straight flat stretch. Refused whole, saying where, if any of it would be outside the box, on something that
  isn't air or replaceable, or touch other redstone: dust of another wire beside it or a diagonal step from it, any
  other redstone beside its dust or its supports (except the two ports' buffer repeaters), or a support placed where
  another wire's climb needs air. Crossing is done by going over: 2 levels up, then down again.
- **`test_redstone` gains `{"show": true}`**: what each `digit` and `number` of the build shows ("the number at
  (x, y, z) shows 16"; a pattern that isn't a digit is "?"), decoded from its segment lamps.
- **Size.** The calculator from gadgets is about 60 wide and 105 deep: it needs the Epic size (128). The system prompt
  says so; at a smaller size Claude says it needs Epic and builds nothing (ruled in the ledger).

## Review Focus

1. **A gadget next to another or to a wire**: nothing of one ever powers the other except through a port. → Task 5
   `aWireBesideAGadgetIsRefused`, and every gadget test places two gadgets side by side at their closest.
2. **Any facing**: a gadget turned right, back or left works exactly as forward. → every gadget test runs 4 facings.
3. **Timing**: a test that waits a gadget's stated settle ticks reads it settled. → every gadget test waits exactly the
   stated settle ticks before reading.
4. **Undo and refinement of a calculator**: undo leaves nothing (no dust, repeater or torch); a refinement's new
   gadgets wire to the old ones. → Task 6 `undoingTheCalculatorLeavesNothing`, `aRefinementWiresToEarlierGadgets`.
5. **Bad options and impossible wires** are refused with what to change, never half built. → Task 2
   `badOptionsAreRefused`, Task 5 `aWireThatCantFitIsRefusedWhole`.
